# 14 — Limitations (read before quoting any number)

## Evidence type
1. **No own orders were placed.** No slippage, fill probability or adverse selection in this study is *realised* execution; all empirical "slippage" is the **book-walk cost of a hypothetical order on a public snapshot**, and all fill numbers are **bounds** computed from public trades vs displayed queue. The only ground truth for execution costs is the synthetic world, which is constructed by the author. (Doing better requires exchange testnets/small live orders — out of scope and prohibited by `EXTERNAL_RESEARCH_ONLY`.)
2. **Synthetic circularity.** Where a model shares the truth generator's functional form (book walk on an exactly observed book, propagator with the true kernel) it wins by construction. Only the misspecified scenarios (`gap`, `withdraw`, `wall`, `concave_book`, `fragmented`, wrong kernel, truncated depth) carry evidential weight. The synthetic book (β = 1.5) is *more convex* than the live top-of-book (β ≈ 0.3–0.7, `05`); the `concave_book` scenario was added for that reason but the calibration of other parameters (λ, φ, τ0, γ, ρ) is illustrative only.
3. **Small, single-regime samples.** Live capture: ~50 minutes on one day (2026-09-29), 3 assets × 3 venues, 25 visible levels (plus an 8-minute 400-level capture); Binance Vision: 4 (BTC) / 2 (ETH) days of aggTrades, 3–4 months of funding and klines. No day/night/weekend or stress-event coverage; no claim about stress liquidity (the 2-second polling cannot see sub-second book flicker).
4. **Sampling artefacts.** REST polling at ≈ 2–2.6 s with 80–290 ms round trips: (a) snapshot-based **effective/realized spreads are dominated by mid staleness** when the quoted spread is a fraction of a bp (measured effective ≫ quoted; see `06`); (b) trade↔quote alignment ignores exchange↔local clock skew; (c) snapshot-to-snapshot walk differences include real book flicker *and* sampling; (d) the queue bound ignores cancellations.
5. **Trade-side conventions.** Aggressor side was taken from the venue flag where the flag semantics are documented (OKX, Kraken taker side) and *inferred from the quote rule agreement rate* for Coinbase, whose REST flag agrees with the quote rule only ~30 % (i.e. it is the maker side; used inverted). Agreement rates are reported in `06`; residual misclassification is unquantified.
6. **Quote currency.** OKX BTC-USDT vs Coinbase/Kraken BTC-USD: ~2 bps constant USDT/USD basis removed in cross-venue tables; cross-venue "edges" are quote-skew-prone (1.5–3 s alignment) and are **not** arbitrage evidence.
7. **Unofficial/secondary data.** FX/gold/WTI OHLC from an unofficial public chart endpoint (no quotes, no volume for FX); continuous futures series contain roll artefacts (WTI gap statistics inflated). Fee schedules for OKX/Bybit/Coinbase are UNKNOWN (secondary sources only); OKX margin-borrow rate unit is unverified.
8. **Literature formulas coded from notes.** Roll, Corwin–Schultz, Abdi–Ranaldo, Almgren–Chriss cost form, square-root law, propagator kernel were coded from formulas quoted in the landscape notes (many tagged `[M]`, i.e. from memory) and validated only on synthetic data. Constants may differ from the papers' exact definitions. The OSS `bidask` package (EDGE) is an independent implementation and behaves consistently with the qualitative findings.
9. **Not tested at all:** VWAP/POV scheduling, VPIN, queue-reactive/Cont–Stoikov–Talreja models (need L3/event data: `MODEL_NOT_EMPIRICALLY_CALIBRATABLE_FROM_FREE_DATA`), FX/gold/commodity quote-based costs, borrow rates, swap points, COMEX/CME rolls, exchange matching-engine specifics (self-trade prevention, iceberg, post-only rules), fees/rebates tiers, taxes.
10. **Statistics.** Bias is reported with Monte-Carlo standard errors in the synthetic tables (`IS_se`, seed stability); the live tables report point estimates without confidence intervals (n small, autocorrelated snapshots). No multiple-comparison correction. Treat differences < ~0.1 bps in the live tables as noise.
11. **What "supported" means here.** A model is called supported only for the data regime and scenario range in which it was executed and not falsified; nothing here licenses using it in production or inside AurumShift. Those are later local PAPER evaluations (mission scope).

## Things that could overturn conclusions
* A real-fill dataset (even a few hundred small orders) showing book-walk bias > the stale-snapshot noise measured here.
* Evidence that the live top-of-book concavity persists beyond 25 levels (then the sqrt law's convex assumption is worse, not better).
* Stress periods (liquidity withdrawal, funding spikes) — synthetic `withdraw` and `gap` show these dominate the tails and no evaluated model predicts them.
